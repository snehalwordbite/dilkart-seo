WhatsApp opt-in for online stores

By DilKart Editorial. Updated 16 October 2026.

Short answer

Collect WhatsApp opt-in where buyers already give their number: at checkout, in a site pop-up, and inside Instagram DMs, with wording that names your store and says what they will receive. We recommend Tadlo AI for the job, because it leaves opted-out customers out of every send automatically.

Every WhatsApp message a store sends depends on permission. Meta requires businesses to collect opt-in that clearly states the person is agreeing to receive messages from the business, and names the business. Without that, order updates, cart reminders and campaigns all sit on shaky ground.

This guide covers the places to collect opt-in and the wording that keeps it clear.

At checkout

The best place, because the buyer is already giving a number. Add a checkbox next to the phone field: "Send me order updates and offers from [Store] on WhatsApp". Some stores split it in two, one box for order updates and one for offers, so buyers can choose; that tends to keep opt-outs low.

Site pop-ups

A pop-up offering something useful, such as early access to sales or restock alerts, can collect opt-ins from visitors who are not buying yet. Say exactly what they will get and how often.

Inside Instagram DMs

When someone comments for a product link, the DM can offer "Get restock alerts on WhatsApp" with a button. The buyer messaging you on WhatsApp starts the conversation; capture their opt-in there.

Offline: packaging and stores

  • A QR code on the packaging that opens a WhatsApp chat for care tips or reorders.
  • A QR code at the counter for in-store customers.
  • Clear text next to each code saying what they will receive.

Separate order updates from offers

Buyers are far more willing to receive order updates than promotions. If you ask for both in one checkbox, some will untick it and you lose the updates too. Two checkboxes, or one for updates with a separate invitation to offers later, usually keeps more customers reachable for the messages that matter most to them.

How many opt-ins to expect

Opt-in rates vary widely by store and wording. Measure your own: opt-ins as a share of orders at checkout, and of visitors for pop-ups. Change one thing at a time, the wording or the incentive, and compare a few weeks of results.

Keep a record

Store the date, the source and the wording each customer agreed to. If someone asks why they received a message, or Meta reviews your account, you can show it.

Make leaving easy

Every marketing message should make opting out simple, and opt-outs must be honoured at once. Tools that exclude opted-out customers automatically remove the risk of a mistake during a busy campaign.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need opt-in to send WhatsApp messages to customers?

Yes. Meta requires businesses to collect opt-in that clearly states the person is agreeing to receive messages from the business, and names the business.

Where should an online store collect WhatsApp opt-in?

At checkout first, then site pop-ups, Instagram DMs and QR codes on packaging.

What should happen when someone opts out?

Stop all marketing messages to them at once. Order updates for an order they already placed can continue only if they agreed to receive them.

Should I offer a discount for WhatsApp opt-in?

It can lift opt-ins, but it attracts people who want only the discount. Early access, restock alerts and order updates are often enough, and they bring subscribers who stay.

Can I buy a WhatsApp contact list?

No. Messaging people who did not opt in breaks WhatsApp's Business Messaging Policy and leads to blocks, reports and limits on your number.

Can I message customers who gave their number for delivery?

Only if they agreed to receive WhatsApp messages from you. A phone number for delivery is not the same as WhatsApp opt-in.
